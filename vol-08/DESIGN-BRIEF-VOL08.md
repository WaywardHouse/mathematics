# Volume 8 Design Brief

## Mandate

Volume 8 should read like the mathematics of upper-year engineering practice,
not like a second pass through a general advanced engineering mathematics
textbook. Volume 7 installs the objects. Volume 8 shows what those objects
become when they are embedded in discipline courses, computational workflows,
and design decisions.

The guiding curricular question is:

> What mathematics does a U of A engineering student need once the dedicated
> math courses are over but the mathematical thinking has become more demanding?

## Core curriculum build

The proposed sequence is:

1. Control, feedback, and stability
2. Discrete-time systems and signal processing
3. Continuum mechanics and transport
4. Computational methods for engineering models
5. Estimation, inverse problems, and filtering
6. Reliability, stochastic systems, and quality
7. Nonlinear optimisation for design and operations

This sequence deliberately covers the mathematical moves that recur across
third- and fourth-year engineering: modelling dynamics, sampling and filtering,
writing conservation laws, discretising field equations, inferring hidden
states from measurements, quantifying failure and risk, and making decisions in
nonlinear constrained spaces.

## U of A alignment

Use University of Alberta upper-year engineering courses as anchoring contexts,
not as chapter silos. The goal is not to mirror any one programme calendar but
to cover the mathematical structures those courses repeatedly demand.

Recurring anchors:

- control and system response
- continuous- and discrete-time signals
- transport and diffusion
- simulation on meshes and computational grids
- regression, estimation, filtering, and data assimilation
- quality, reliability, and queueing
- nonlinear design optimisation

## Optional viewpoints

Each chapter should carry at least one optional viewpoint box from outside the
engineering home field. Keep them mathematically honest and structurally
parallel to the engineering case.

Recommended viewpoint set:

- Hard sciences: field laws, waves, inverse problems, and measurement
- Computing and data: filtering, optimisation, signal processing, learned models
- Finance and quant: stochastic evolution, estimation, optimisation under risk
- Geography and environment: transport, remote sensing, hydrology, spatial risk

The optional viewpoints are there to widen transfer, not to dilute the core.

## Visual design

Vol 8 should be visibly more systems-oriented than Vol 7. Prefer:

- block diagrams, signal-flow graphs, and feedback loops
- state portraits and phase trajectories
- mesh and control-volume diagrams
- before/after filtering views of noisy signals
- parameter-sensitivity and tradeoff surfaces
- uncertainty bands, posterior updates, and reliability curves

The visual thread should make one point repeatedly: upper-year mathematics is
not more abstract because it is farther from reality. It is more abstract
because reality has become more coupled.
